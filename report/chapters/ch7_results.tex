\chapter{Results}\label{ch:results}

Every number in this chapter is read from a file in the repository; the relevant result folder is named in each section. Results are reported per dimension first and the aggregate FRIES score last, in line with the central framing. We separate three layers wherever the data allow: the \emph{evidence} (what the probe measured), the \emph{risk decision} (whether TrustLens raised a risk, a gap, or abstained) and the \emph{score}. Much of what we learned lives in the differences between those layers.

\section{Pilot: first end-to-end experiment}\label{sec:pilot}
The first experiment (14--15 September 2026, \code{results/\allowbreak flawed\_model\_suite/\allowbreak eval\_results*}) evaluated \code{unitary/\allowbreak toxic-bert} and six forged variants twice, with the first probe set, in which Safety and Explainability read the model card only. We use it here for what it taught us, not for its numbers.

\paragraph{The reference model was a constant predictor.} The first evaluation of the Hub import of \code{toxic-bert} returned a demographic-parity difference, equalized-odds difference and F1 spread of exactly zero, with clean accuracy 0.7987. The stored per-group statistics show a positive-prediction rate of 0.0 in \emph{both} groups: the model never predicted ``toxic''. Since 20\% of the sample is toxic, always answering ``not toxic'' scores 0.80, and the model scored 0.799. The cause was the label mapping. \code{toxic-bert} has six sigmoid outputs; our contract sent dataset value~1 to output index~1, which is \code{severe\_toxic}, and a classifier that fires on severe toxicity only will say ``no'' to almost everything. The evaluation contract had stored the snapshot of the six labels, which is how we found the problem after the fact. TrustLens had scored this model as nicely fair and nicely robust, which is what a degenerate classifier looks like to a probe that checks only stability. That is why the prediction-collapse gate (threshold 0.98) exists, and why the 2-label reference was built.

\paragraph{After the correction.} With the 2-label reference (accuracy 0.868, DPD 0.283 with 95\% CI [0.243, 0.324], EOD 0.174) the targeted-dimension comparison held for five of six variants in the pilot, but the Safety variant V5 scored 3.98 on Safety against the reference's 3.91, because the probe at that time only read the card. The overall FRIES score of the corrected reference was 6.16, and three variants scored higher than that.

\paragraph{The LLM-rated dimensions moved.} Run~1 and Run~2 differed only in Integrity, Explainability and Safety. The original reference's overall score fell from 6.79 to 5.44 between runs while its card and metadata were identical; the other six models moved by 0.05--0.49 (\code{report/\allowbreak figures/\allowbreak numbers.txt}).

Three consequences carried into the rest of the project: a constant-predictor gate, a behavioural Safety probe, and repeated runs with a frozen LLM configuration.

\section{Controlled suite: repeatability}\label{sec:repeat}
The main suite (nine models, five runs each, \code{results/\allowbreak flawed\_model\_suite/\allowbreak final\_20261002\_*}, methodology \code{v3-hardening-2026}) first answers RQ1.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Deterministic dimensions.} Fairness, Robustness and behavioural Safety scores have a standard deviation of \textbf{0.000} over five runs for every model (27 model-by-dimension cells).
\item \textbf{LLM-rated dimensions.} Integrity and Explainability had a mean standard deviation of 0.64 and a maximum of 2.23 (on a control model). The FRIES standard deviation per model ranged from 0.00 to 0.59.
\item \textbf{Evidence-level flags} agreed across runs in 100\% of cases.
\item \textbf{Provider reliability.} In 34 of 45 runs the LLM answered; 11 of 45 fell back to the heuristic (provider rate limits, and three DNS failures). On the real-model run, 14 of 15 fell back because the provider's daily token quota (200\,000 tokens per day) was exhausted.
\end{itemize}
Measured evidence is therefore reproducible to the last digit. The dimensions that depend on an LLM are noisier, and they depend on a provider's quota.

Monotonicity violations were zero everywhere, and mapping failures were zero. Under v3, robustness $O$ was constant (9) and safety $S$ was constant (6), so those levels carried no ordering; under v4 both $O$ and $S$ have $\rho=-1.0$ with no ties on all three families. $D$ is constant (8) in every family under both mappings, so it contributes nothing to ordering. The controls moved too: the Fairness reference (gap 0.145, \code{F-FAIR-EOPP} fired) went from 8.65 under v3 to 7.32 under v4, and the Safety reference (severe FNR 0.40) stayed at 6.60.

\paragraph{What score calibration does not show.} A wider range is not better calibration: the v4 anchors were fitted to the span of the injected defects, so the 4.60--6.65 range is largely what the fit is built to produce. It shows monotone separation of synthetic severity levels, not that a score of 2.0 matches real-world harm or any external judgment of trustworthiness. It tests generalisation across seeds and data draws, not across tasks or architectures, and $\rho=-1.0$ on three to five points is descriptive (Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}). The two lowest fairness levels are lost to the CI-width gate, not the mapping. The default deterministic engine still abstains, so v4 changes only the \code{legacy\_heuristic} and \code{llm\_v1} baselines.

\paragraph{Where the information is lost.} At the evidence layer nothing is lost ($\rho=1.0$ in all three families). The \emph{risk} layer carries no graded signal for Robustness or Safety at all: \code{R-ROB-PERT} never fires in the sweep, and Safety has no behavioural risk identifier. That does not change under v4 (Table~\ref{tab:threelayer} summarises the three layers per dimension).

\section{Artifact integrity}\label{sec:l7}
Artifact integrity was an implementation and design change; we ran no new statistical experiment for it, so our evidence is small and we say so.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tamper demonstration} (\code{tamper\_demo.json}). Untouched V1 weights compared with an operator-supplied reference gave \code{match}. Flipping a single byte changed the sha256 (from \code{5918a851\ldots} to \code{74c230bb\ldots}), the comparison became \code{diverge} and \code{I-INT-BYTES-DIVERGE} was raised.
\item \textbf{Hub models.} In the 2026-10-02 real-model run, 15 of 15 models gave \code{match} between the cached weight bytes and the Hub's LFS sha256. In the v9 external run, all seven were VERIFIED.
\item \textbf{A real provenance finding.} \code{textdetox/\allowbreak xlmr-large-toxicity-classifier} and \code{textdetox/\allowbreak xlmr-base-toxicity-classifier} ship byte-identical weights (sha256 \code{829a9d93\ldots}, hidden size 768, 12 layers). The ``large'' repository does not contain large-size weights. Hash matching against the Hub verifies that each repository's bytes are what the Hub says they are, and says nothing about whether the name is accurate; this finding came from comparing the hashes of two repositories, which the probe did not do on its own.
\item \textbf{Independent-review packets.} Packets 08 (tampered V1 against its manifest) and 09 (V1 against its own manifest) test the mismatch and the control (Section~\ref{sec:l10}).
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:integ} lists what artifact integrity does not prove.

\section{Deterministic versus LLM interpretation}\label{sec:l2}
\paragraph{Run status.} The text below reports the provider-only result; the 60/60 completion follows. Sixty LLM runs were planned (12 packets, five runs each, 36 packet-by-dimension pairs; \code{results/\allowbreak l2\_det\_vs\_llm\_20261003}). Only 22 returned a genuine answer (provider A 9, provider B 13); the other 38 (fallback rate 0.63) were provider or infrastructure failures with no model text. There were no parser failures or output truncations; five prompts were truncated because the P01 card exceeds 5\,000 characters. Provider attempts over both sessions: provider A 9 ok, 12 times 503 (high demand) and 80 times 429 (quota); provider B 13 ok and 82 times 429 (tokens per day, limit 200\,000); provider C 0 ok and 79 times 404 (``Function not found''). The provider C failures are a configuration defect, a model endpoint that no longer exists, not a quota problem. Only P01--P04 (five runs each), P05 (one) and P09 (one) were answered; P06--P08 and P10--P12 got no LLM answer at all.

\subsection{Human evidence review}
The final human study (\code{results/\allowbreak l10\_human\_analysis\_20261005}) comes from a Google Form with the 12 blinded packets. Seven submissions were received, so $7\times12=84$ answer sets. Reviewers are evidence reviewers, not ground truth, and the analysis is descriptive. The raw CSV export has sha256 \code{da680bbc\ldots}, and re-running our analysis script reproduces the committed report exactly.

\paragraph{Respondents and data quality.} Six submissions carry the shared code \code{R0} (numbered R0-1 to R0-6 by timestamp; they may be different people), and one carries \code{R2}. Both groups are analysed as given, and a sensitivity set without R2 is reported alongside. \textbf{R2 is low quality after the first packet}: 9 of 12 rationales have at most ten characters (e.g.\ ``NA'', ``yes'', ``3''), three rationales are the same pasted sentence, one answers Q2 = NO while the rationale asserts ``a meaningful disparity'', and two pair a ``yes'' or ``good'' with severity 1 (catastrophic). R2's median rationale is 6 characters and its Q3 matched the packet's dimension in 6 of 12.

\begin{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats that change how the table should be read.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The O/S/D source differs between models.} Providers A and B answered for the first three models; for the other four the LLM providers were unavailable and the heuristic fallback was used (recorded per aspect). Where an Explainability score exists, 3.30 and 2.88 come from an LLM and 1.00--6.00 from the heuristic; these are not like for like, and neither are the Integrity scores. The behavioural Safety band always comes from the heuristic.
\item \textbf{Risk layer and score layer disagree.} \code{xlmr-large} and the dynabench model have Robustness scores of 2.00 although the risk layer calls the first of them \code{no\_material\_risk} (drop 0.054, CI lower bound 0.043, below the 0.05 tolerance). The v4 scale is range-normalised to our injected defect range (Section~\ref{sec:v4}), so a drop of about five points maps to the floor of the scale, while the risk rule needs the CI lower bound to clear $\varepsilon_{drop}$. Neither is wrong by its own definition, and they should not be read as one judgment.
\item \textbf{One run per model.} Evidence is deterministic, but the LLM-sourced numbers are not repeated. The earlier v8 run with 7 of these models is left untouched and not compared here.
\item \textbf{Selection.} The seven models were chosen to vary, not sampled. Nothing about their order or scores supports a statement about the Hub.
\end{itemize}
