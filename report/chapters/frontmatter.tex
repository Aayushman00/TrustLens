\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Downloading a classifier from a public model hub takes one line of code, but deciding whether it deserves to be used is harder: the information at hand is a free-text model card, a possibly empty licence field and no standard evidence about behaviour on sensitive groups. \textbf{TrustLens} is a semi-automated research prototype that collects this evidence for Hugging Face text-classification models and organises it around the five FRIES dimensions: Fairness, Robustness, Integrity, Explainability and Safety. It imports a model, runs five automated tests (probes) locally, stores the evidence in files named by their SHA-256 hashes, converts it into Occurrence/Severity/Detection (O/S/D) ratings, three scales borrowed from Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA), and produces a versioned report. The primary output is a five-dimensional audit profile; the aggregate FRIES score is a secondary summary. The behavioural experiments cover one task, English-language toxicity classification on the Civil Comments dataset.

We evaluated the system with controlled flawed models, experiments with progressively larger injected defects, a fairness confirmation planned before it was run, a comparison with interpretation by a large language model (LLM), a human evidence review and a descriptive audit of seven Hub models. Measured evidence from the deterministic probes was identical across five repeated runs and changed consistently with the injected defect level in every experiment that returned a measurement. A scoring method scaled to the range of the injected defects widened the score range from 0.68--1.18 to 4.60--6.65 points. Separating missing documentation from evidence of an observed problem removed false flags on clean controls, and a rule on the gap in true-positive rates between two groups (the equal-opportunity difference) flagged every injected disparity at label-flip rates of 0.4 and above on fresh data. Several results were negative: the prespecified robustness sweep withheld a rating at every level, the fairness rule also flagged the real reference model, and the comparison with LLM interpretation was inconclusive (all 60 planned calls returned valid responses, 22 in the first sessions and 38 additional valid responses in the final dataset, from three providers, five of them on a shortened model card; status agreement with the deterministic layer was 11 of 36 packet--dimension pairs). The scores separate synthetic defect levels but are not calibrated to real-world harm; the fairness groups come from an annotator-reported identity-attack signal, with no mention-based rerun; the file-integrity check shows agreement with a reference hash for the weight file the worker loaded (one file per Hub model in the external audit), not that a model is safe or free of backdoors; and the human review and Hub audit are exploratory and descriptive (Section~\ref{ch:limits}).

TrustLens is meant to help a human reviewer see where the evidence is strong and where it is thin.

\textbf{Keywords:} model auditing, trustworthy AI, FRIES, Hugging Face Hub, fairness, equal opportunity, artifact integrity, model cards, text classification.
