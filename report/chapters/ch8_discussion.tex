Three things stand out. First, the evidence layer is the strongest part of the system: it is reproducible, it tracks severity, and it catches things that metadata cannot, such as a byte flip. Second, the risk layer is conservative by construction, since a risk is only raised on positive evidence of a problem and above a stated tolerance. That conservatism produced the largest systematic difference from human reviewers (Section~\ref{sec:disc-human}). Third, the score layer is where the arbitrariness lives, since this is where a proposal turns evidence into a rating, and every one of our fixes there (v4, v9) made a defensible choice that could have gone another way. We think anyone who builds a FRIES tool will meet this same step and should expect it to be the hardest one to defend.

\section{The fairness rule}
Fairness took four attempts. The first binary path could not raise a risk. The second (excess DPD) failed its own inspection because it depended on base rates. The TPR-gap rule that replaced it detected every injected defect at flip rates of 0.4 and above on fresh data (H2, H3). It also flagged the real reference model (H1), whose own disparity (0.145) is close to the lowest injected one (EOD 0.167 at flip 0.4). Because the Civil Comments identity groups have base rates of 0.11 against 0.64, a disparity cannot be attributed to the injection, the data or the model from predictions alone, so the rule's status text describes it as ``evidence for review''. The CI-width gate (0.15) was inherited and not retuned.

\section{Human reviewers and TrustLens}\label{sec:disc-human}
Humans agree with each other on sufficiency and dimension, and less on risk and severity (Krippendorff's $\alpha=0.60$ on severity). The main disagreement with TrustLens (Table~\ref{tab:l10tl}) is consistent: on four packets (P04, P06, P07, P10) the evidence layer measured a severe-harm miss rate and the risk layer raised no risk, while the human plurality did. The studied version (v9) had no behavioural safety risk rule, which is what the v10 rule addresses. The packets do not define an acceptable miss rate, so when behaviour becomes a risk is a policy choice; reports therefore show the measured number next to a reference. In the other direction, TrustLens abstained on two packets (P02, P09) through the CI-width and listing-unverified gates, so abstention rates should be reported together with the gate values.

\section{Deterministic versus LLM interpretation}
We kept the deterministic engine as the default. Where the LLM answered, the exact $O/S/D$ triple repeated in none of the 12 provider pairs (3 of 24 for the supplementary evaluator), and answers depended on the provider reached (Integrity $O$ 5.67 from one provider, 2.85 from the other). The LLM does read card prose and noticed content problems that a keyword section detector cannot, such as a card with the right headings and no content. We therefore treat \code{llm\_v1} as an experimental comparison engine, report which source rated each aspect, and do not claim either engine is better.

\section{Why the aggregate score is secondary}
The aggregate FRIES number is a function of ratings that are proposals, combined by a geometric mean with equal weights. Our data give four direct reasons not to read it as more than a summary.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It moves with the LLM.} In the pilot the original reference's overall score fell 1.34 points between two runs with identical inputs.
\item \textbf{It hides abstention.} A model with a withheld dimension has no score, while a model whose dimension was filled by the fallback has one. Two scores can therefore differ in how much of the evidence stands behind them (Table~\ref{tab:hubprof}).
\item \textbf{Its scale is relative.} Under v4 a three-point accuracy drop is the bottom of the scale; under v3 the same drop (eight swaps) scored 7.96. Neither number says how bad three points is.
\item \textbf{It cannot be validated here.} We have no ground truth for trust. The reviewer study checks evidence reading, not the score.
\end{itemize}
For these reasons the report shows the profile first. The profile can be read dimension by dimension, and the evidence behind each entry can be inspected.
