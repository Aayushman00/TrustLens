\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{What we built and what we found}
TrustLens is an evidence-driven, FRIES-aligned model auditing framework. Its primary output is a five-dimensional audit profile in which each entry points back to stored, hashed evidence, and in which abstention is a first-class result. The aggregate FRIES score is a secondary heuristic summary, computed from proposed ratings, and we do not offer it as a validated measure of trustworthiness.

We tested the system in ten rounds; Table~\ref{tab:rq} gives the answer to each research question. The findings we are most confident of are modest and concrete. The measured evidence is reproducible and rises monotonically with injected severity ($\rho=1.0$ in every sweep). The layers above it are where the problems were: the first O/S/D bands compressed the severity range into 1.3--1.7 points, the documentation-based risk rule flagged every clean control until disclosure gaps were separated from risks, and the final equal-opportunity rule detected every injected fairness defect at flip rates of 0.4 and above on fresh data but also flagged the real reference model. Artifact-set integrity detects byte changes against a manifest and found one real provenance oddity, without saying anything about the safety of the verified bytes. Independent reviewers agree with TrustLens on risk for 6 of 12 packets, and most of the disagreement has one cause: the studied version of TrustLens raised no behavioural safety risk and reviewers did. This is not a validation of TrustLens.

\section{What we do not conclude}
The work does not show that TrustLens can tell whether an arbitrary model is trustworthy, or that the FRIES aggregate means anything outside this setup: one task, one dataset family, mostly BERT-family models, synthetic defects and a small, partly non-independent reviewer sample (Chapter~\ref{ch:limits}). We would like readers to take away a method as much as a result: separate the layers, declare before running, freeze the folders, report the failures.

\section{Future work}
In rough order of how much we think each would improve the work:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Validate the decision layer on new data}, including the v10 safety rule (pre-registered tolerance, a sweep not used so far) and its competence floor.
\item \textbf{A larger, independent reviewer study} with distinct identifiers, domain experts, packets not chosen by the authors, and an ethics review.
\item \textbf{Repeat the LLM comparison with one pinned provider} (ideally local), so all 60 runs come from one model.
\item \textbf{More than one risk per dimension}, with mapping traces, as the FRIES procedure intends.
\item \textbf{Broader probes and provenance:} gradient-guided perturbations, SHAP or LIME, intersectional groups, other tasks; signed manifests and cross-repository hashes.\end{enumerate}
