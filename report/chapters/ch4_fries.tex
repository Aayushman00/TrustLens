\chapter{FRIES Methodology}\label{ch:fries}

This chapter defines what TrustLens measures and how evidence becomes risk decisions, ratings and scores, as used in the experiments. Only the method variants needed to read a result are named; version history and rule identifiers are in \code{docs/REPRODUCING\_THESIS\_RESULTS.md}.

\section{Four stages between a measurement and a score}\label{sec:stages}
A measurement becomes a score in four separate steps, which the experiments of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} keep apart.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Measured evidence} is what a probe computed from the model and the data: an accuracy, a gap in true-positive rates with its interval, a set of file hashes, the share of card sections present. It is a number or a status and carries no judgment.
\item \textbf{Risk findings and documentation gaps} are decisions taken from the evidence by fixed rules with stated thresholds. The outcome is one of: a \emph{risk} (positive evidence of a problem), a \emph{disclosure gap} (information that should be present is missing), no material finding, or \emph{insufficient evidence}, in which case the later stages are skipped.
\item \textbf{Proposed ratings} are the O/S/D triples that an engine suggests or a reviewer enters. They are proposals; the report labels them as requiring validation.
\item \textbf{Dimension and aggregate scores} are computed from the ratings by the FRIES formula (Equations~\ref{eq:pi} and~\ref{eq:fries}) and exist only where a complete triple exists.
\end{enumerate}

A change at one stage does not automatically propagate to the next: a parity gap that rises from 0.11 to 0.21 changes the evidence at every step, changes the risk finding only when the rule's threshold is crossed (Table~\ref{tab:fairrule}), and changes the rating only if the mapping assigns a different integer.

\section{From ratings to scores}
FRIES \cite{rutinowski2024} adapts FMEA. It covers five dimensions: \emph{Fairness} (does behaviour differ between groups of texts), \emph{Robustness} (does it survive small typos), \emph{Integrity} (are the evaluated files and the model's identity what is claimed), \emph{Explainability} (does the documentation explain the model) and \emph{Safety} (does the model miss severe harmful content, and does its card disclose safety limits). For each relevant risk an analyst rates \emph{Occurrence} (O: how unlikely the failure is), \emph{Severity} (S: how mild its consequences would be) and \emph{Detection} (D: how easily the failure would be noticed before it causes harm). TrustLens uses an inverted convention in which \textbf{higher is more favourable on all three scales}, so $9/9/8$ is a favourable rating. Conventional FMEA ranks the other way, with high numbers for frequent, severe or hard-to-detect failures. This convention applies to every engine in the experiments; it does not mean any engine produces validated risk estimates (Section~\ref{sec:osd}). The scorer requires integers in $0..10$ and
\begin{align}
\Pi_r &= \sqrt[3]{O_r\, S_r\, D_r}, \qquad \Pi_r = 0 \text{ if any of } O_r,S_r,D_r = 0 \text{ (veto)},\label{eq:pi}\\
T_a &= \frac{1}{|R_a|}\sum_{r\in R_a}\Pi_r, \qquad
\text{FRIES} = \sum_{a} w_a\, T_a, \quad w_a = 0.2 \text{ by default.}\label{eq:fries}
\end{align}
In every experiment each dimension has a single triple, so $T_a=\Pi_r$ and weights are equal. The geometric mean lets one low component dominate. The rule-based engine proposes only $1..9$ (0 and 10 are reserved for human judgment), so its dimension scores lie between $1.0$ and $9.0$.

\section{Evidence: the five dimensions}
\subsection{Fairness}\label{sec:fair}
For a binary classifier with favourable class $\hat y=1$ and groups $g$ defined by a sensitive column, the probe computes the \emph{demographic-parity difference} (DPD), the gap between groups in the share of texts predicted positive, and, for each group, the \emph{true-positive rate} (TPR), the share of actual positives that are predicted positive. The gap in TPR between groups is the \emph{equal-opportunity difference} (EOD):
\begin{align}
\mathrm{DPD} &= \max_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g)-\min_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g),\\
\mathrm{TPR}_g &= \tfrac{TP_g}{TP_g+FN_g}, \qquad \mathrm{EOD} = \max_g \mathrm{TPR}_g-\min_g \mathrm{TPR}_g.\label{eq:eod}
\end{align}
The probe also reports the corresponding gap in false-positive rates (FPR, the share of actual negatives predicted positive) and the gap in label rates $P(Y{=}1\mid g)$. EOD is the equal-opportunity difference of Hardt et~al.\ \cite{hardt2016}; it is the primary fairness quantity and is distinct from the larger equalized-odds difference (the larger of the TPR and FPR gaps). Groups smaller than 30 are excluded.

\emph{Why the trigger is the TPR gap.} A first rule based on ``excess'' demographic parity depended on group base rates $P(Y{=}1\mid g)$: it was non-zero on clean controls and shrank as the injected defect grew. The TPR gap conditions on the true label. It was chosen after inspecting exploratory data, so it was confirmed on fresh data with a new seed (Section~\ref{sec:fairconf}). The tolerance $\varepsilon=0.02$, the largest TPR gap treated as negligible, is a method-wide practical tolerance and was not tuned.

Uncertainty is expressed as a 95\% \emph{confidence interval} (CI), an interval produced by a procedure designed to cover the true value in 95\% of repeated samples (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Here it is a percentile bootstrap of EOD that re-samples the evaluation rows $B=1000$ times (seed 42). It reflects row resampling on one evaluation set, not retraining of the model. The decision rule (Table~\ref{tab:fairrule}) is a \emph{disparity} rule, not a defect detector: a model with a real TPR gap but no injected defect is flagged, and the report calls this ``evidence for review''. The width rule often decides the outcome because most intervals in our data have width near 0.15; a rating withheld this way is not a pass.

\subsection{Robustness}
The probe replaces up to 3 alphanumeric characters per text (attack budget 0.03) with random characters under a seeded generator and compares clean and perturbed accuracy. Evidence: clean and robust accuracy, paired accuracy drop with bootstrap CI, and perturbation coverage (the share of texts that could be perturbed). A robustness risk is raised when the drop exceeds $\varepsilon_{drop}=0.05$ \emph{and} the CI lower bound does too. A rating is withheld when fewer than 100 samples are evaluated, clean accuracy is below 0.20, perturbation coverage is below 1.0, or the CI of the drop is wider than 0.20. A \emph{constant-predictor gate} refuses to score fairness and robustness when one class receives at least 98\% of predictions, since a broken model is trivially stable. The probe tests typo sensitivity, not adversarial robustness.

\subsection{Safety}\label{sec:safety}
The behavioural part measures how often the model misses \emph{severe} positives: comments whose true label is toxic and whose annotator-averaged severe-toxicity score (dataset column \code{severe\_toxicity}) is above 0.0001. The \emph{severe false-negative rate} (severe FNR) is the share of these comments the model fails to flag as toxic:
\begin{equation}
\text{severe FNR} = \frac{\#\{\text{severe, true positive, predicted negative}\}}{\#\{\text{severe, true positive}\}},
\end{equation}
reported with a Wilson interval (a confidence interval for a proportion that stays reliable for small counts), together with the overall false-negative rate (FNR), their ratio, harmful recall and benign FPR; at least 30 severe positives are required. The threshold is operational: the scores are annotator averages that rarely reach 0.5, so ``above zero'' leaves enough rows. It is not a validated category of severe content, no sensitivity analysis was run, and every severe-FNR value depends on it. In the main experiments the behavioural part produced evidence but no risk, because there is no defensible absolute miss rate at which to raise one. An exploratory severe-miss rule (severe FNR and its Wilson lower bound both above 0.50, benign FPR at most 0.30) was added later and is evaluated only by replay (Section~\ref{sec:l6}). The card part checks four disclosure items (misuse, privacy, security, data); missing items are disclosure gaps.

\subsection{Integrity}\label{sec:integ}
Integrity records whether the model's identity is well specified (exact model version pinned, file listing recorded, licence declared) and whether the bytes evaluated match a stated reference. The probe lists the model's complete \emph{file set} (weights or every shard named by the index, config, generation config, tokenizer files and any other weight-like file) and compares it with the first authoritative \emph{manifest} available, that is, a list of expected files with their reference hashes (operator-supplied, a folder manifest, or a legacy weight hash); for Hub models every file in the manifest is compared with its published sha256. The check covers only the files the manifest names. Outcomes: verified (all match; no risk); mismatch, including an unlisted weight file (a byte-mismatch risk); incomplete (an expected file is missing; rating withheld); unpinned or unsupported (nothing trusted to compare with; not a risk, hash not compared). In the external audit of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} the stored manifest of each Hub model held exactly one weight file, the one the worker loaded (\code{model.safetensors} or \code{pytorch\_model.bin}); other weight formats in the same repositories, such as a second serialisation or an ONNX export, were not hashed. A match shows that the evaluated bytes are the bytes a manifest attests; it does not show who made the manifest, nor that the model is benign, safe or free of backdoors, and a mismatch shows that bytes differ, not that anyone tampered.

\subsection{Explainability}
Explainability means \emph{documentation transparency}. The probe checks the model card for five required sections (intended use, limitations, training data, evaluation, ethical considerations); coverage is the share present, and template placeholders such as ``[More Information Needed]'' do not count. It also detects contradictions such as an open-use claim with a restrictive licence or ``production ready'' with no limitations section (a documentation-contradiction risk). It cannot tell whether a human could understand \emph{why} the model predicted something, and a card with the right headings and empty content can pass the section check.

\section{Risks versus disclosure gaps}\label{sec:gaps}
In the first controlled suite, a detection rule based directly on the measured results flagged both clean controls on Safety and Explainability (rate 1.00) and one on Integrity (0.50). Each flag came from an \emph{absence}: a missing card section, or a local folder with no Hub revision. A detector that fires on every clean model detects nothing. The method therefore keeps two lists. The risk list holds only positive evidence of a problem (a contradiction, a byte mismatch, a metric-based risk). Missing documentation goes to a separate \emph{disclosure-gap} list (missing safety disclosure, incomplete card, unpinned revision, missing manifest, undisclosed licence), and when only gaps exist the dimension is reported as a disclosure gap. A gap can matter for deployment even when no defect has been shown; the split makes the distinction visible and, as Section~\ref{sec:gapres} shows, costs recall on injected card defects.

\section{From evidence to O/S/D ratings}\label{sec:osd}
\textbf{What the three numbers mean in the implementation.} In FMEA an analyst rates three things independently; the TrustLens engines are simpler. For the behavioural dimensions, $O$ and $S$ both come from the same evidence statistic (how far the measurement lies from a defect-free value) and are equal in the range-scaled mapping. $D$ is not how noticeable a failure would be to a user, which the project cannot measure; after the evidence guards it falls as the confidence interval widens. For the card-based dimensions, the three numbers come from card coverage or, in the LLM-assisted engine, from a model prompted to rate each of them. A dimension score is therefore a transformed evidence statistic with an uncertainty discount, not three independent expert judgments.

\textbf{Deterministic engine (default).} It proposes no ratings: O, S and D stay empty and FRIES is withheld unless a reviewer supplies them, because the project has no validated evidence-to-rating mapping.

\textbf{Rule-based engine, original scoring bands.} With $\mathrm{scale}(x)=\mathrm{clamp}(\mathrm{round}(10x),1,9)$: Fairness $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\mathrm{gap})$ with the gap the larger of DPD and the equalized-odds difference, $D=8$; Robustness $O=\mathrm{scale}(\min(\text{robust}/\text{clean},1))$, $S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\min(\text{drop}/0.10,1))$, $D=8$; behavioural Safety $O=\mathrm{scale}(1-\text{severe FNR})$, $S=3$ if the FNR ratio exceeds 1.5 and $6$ otherwise, $D=8$; card-based Integrity, Explainability and Safety $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(\text{pass rate or coverage})$.

\textbf{Range-scaled mapping.}\label{sec:v4} In the first sweeps the evidence changed consistently with injected severity while the dimension score under the original bands moved only 1.3--1.7 points: the bands lose information (robustness $O$ was constant at 9, safety $S$ at 6). For Fairness (evidence $e$ = EOD), Robustness (accuracy drop) and Safety (severe FNR), the range-scaled mapping rescales linearly between two anchors $a$ (expected evidence of a defect-free model) and $b$ (expected evidence at the largest defect used for fitting):
\begin{equation}
x=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{e-a}{b-a},0,1\right),\qquad O=S=\left\lfloor 9-8x+\tfrac12\right\rfloor\in[1,9],\label{eq:v4}
\end{equation}
with $D$, the withholding rules and the card bands as in the original bands. The anchors (Table~\ref{tab:anchors}) are the intercept and the intercept plus slope of an ordinary-least-squares line fitted to the evidence against normalised defect severity on a separate fitting split (Section~\ref{sec:calib}), and were fixed before the evaluation was run.

\noindent The scale is \emph{relative to the largest injected defect}: a robustness drop of three percentage points maps to $O=S=1$, the same as a total failure, because the fitting sweep ended there. Since $O=S$ by construction and $D$ is constant, each dimension is one evidence statistic turned into one number; the score has no external ground truth.

The mapping rests on assumptions. It is linear between the two anchors and saturates at 9 or 1 beyond them. The anchors come from one fitting split, one base model and one training seed per level, with injected label flips and character swaps rather than real defects. The ``worst'' end is the largest defect we injected, so an observed value equal to it scores 1 whether or not it would harm anyone, and the three families are scaled independently, so a Fairness score of 5 and a Safety score of 5 do not describe equal risk. What the mapping supports is the claim tested in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}: it separates the synthetic defect levels we tested. It is not calibrated against real-world harm and has not been validated externally.

\textbf{Later evidence guards.} Three guards were added after the first real-model run: a byte mismatch forces Integrity $O=S=1$; Safety is withheld when the benign FPR exceeds 0.30 (a model flagging everything must not look safe by its miss rate); and $D=\mathrm{clamp}(8-\lfloor w/0.05\rfloor)$ instead of a constant 8, where $w$ is the width of the evidence interval (for Robustness, that of the clean-accuracy interval, not of the drop). The FPR ceiling was set after seeing the external audit, and none of the guards has been validated on fresh data. The controlled suite and severity sweeps (Sections~\ref{sec:repeat}--\ref{sec:l6}) used the original bands (labelled ``v3'' in some figure files), the scoring-method evaluation the range-scaled mapping (``v4''), and the integrity, LLM-comparison, review and Hub results (Sections~\ref{sec:l7}--\ref{sec:hub}) the final method with the evidence guards (``v9''). The labels only name these three stored method versions.

\textbf{LLM-assisted engine.} For Integrity, Explainability and Safety (card part) one batched prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}) asks a hosted language model for O, S, D in $1..9$ with a rationale. The prompt is shared by the providers (A, B and C, Section~\ref{sec:l2}), but sampling settings are provider-specific: temperature 0.2 was recorded for providers B and C, and the temperature used by provider A was not recorded (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}). If every provider fails, the rule-based ratings are kept as a tagged \emph{fallback}. The engine is experimental, and Section~\ref{sec:l2} shows why it is not a validated rater.
