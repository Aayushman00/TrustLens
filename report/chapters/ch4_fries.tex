\chapter{FRIES Methodology}\label{ch:fries}

This chapter explains how TrustLens turns a model and a dataset into an audit profile. The order follows the data: first the arithmetic that is borrowed from the FRIES paper, then each probe, then the two decisions that sit between measurement and score (what counts as a \emph{risk}, and how a risk becomes O/S/D). Version labels such as \code{v4-disclosure-gaps-2026} are the methodology versions stamped on evaluations; Table~\ref{tab:versions} lists them.

\section{From evidence to score}
FRIES, as proposed by Rutinowski et~al.\ \cite{rutinowski2024}, adapts FMEA to trust. For each relevant risk an analyst rates \emph{Occurrence} (O), \emph{Severity} (S) and \emph{Detection} (D). TrustLens follows the convention that \textbf{higher is better on all three scales}: a high O means the failure is unlikely, a high S means the consequence is mild, a high D means it is easy to notice. Mixing this up with ordinary FMEA, where high means dangerous, would invert the result, so the scorer validates that all three are integers in $0..10$.

The arithmetic in \code{scoring/\allowbreak fries.py} is short enough to state completely:
\begin{align}
\Pi_r &= \sqrt[3]{O_r\, S_r\, D_r}, \qquad \Pi_r = 0 \text{ if any of } O_r,S_r,D_r = 0 \text{ (veto)},\label{eq:pi}\\
T_a &= \frac{1}{|R_a|}\sum_{r\in R_a}\Pi_r, \qquad
\text{FRIES} = \sum_{a} w_a\, T_a, \quad w_a = 0.2 \text{ by default.}\label{eq:fries}
\end{align}
Custom weights must each be at least $0.1$ and sum to one. In every experiment in this report each dimension has a single O/S/D triple, so $T_a=\Pi_r$ and the weights are equal.

The geometric mean has a consequence worth stating. One low component pulls $\Pi$ down much more than an arithmetic mean would: $(O,S,D)=(1,2,2)$ gives $\Pi=\sqrt[3]{4}=1.59$, whereas $(9,9,8)$ gives $8.65$. It also means that the heuristic engine, which never proposes 0 or 10, produces dimension scores between $1.0$ and $9.0$.

\paragraph{Profile first, score second.} A report lists, for each dimension, the probe status, the measured evidence, any risk or disclosure gap, the O/S/D triple with its source (heuristic, LLM, human, or abstained) and only then the dimension score. The aggregate FRIES figure is placed last and is \emph{withheld} when any dimension has no complete O/S/D triple. TrustLens keeps (A) measured evidence, (B) risk assessment and (C) the score apart (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}); the heuristic engine comes close to blurring A and C, because each of its bands is a function of one metric (Section~\ref{sec:osd}).

\section{Fairness}\label{sec:fair}
\textbf{What is measured.} For a binary classifier with positive class $\hat y=\code{positive\_label\_index}$ and groups $g$ defined by the sensitive column, the probe computes (all in \code{fairness\_metrics.py}):
\begin{align}
\mathrm{DPD} &= \max_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g)-\min_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g),\\
\mathrm{TPR}_g &= \tfrac{TP_g}{TP_g+FN_g}, \qquad \mathrm{EOD} = \max_g \mathrm{TPR}_g-\min_g \mathrm{TPR}_g,\label{eq:eod}\\
\text{FPR gap} &= \max_g \mathrm{FPR}_g-\min_g \mathrm{FPR}_g,\\
\text{label-rate gap} &= \max_g P(Y{=}1\mid g)-\min_g P(Y{=}1\mid g).
\end{align}
The field \code{equalized\_odds\_difference} is the larger of the TPR gap and the FPR gap. In this report ``EOD'' always means the TPR gap of Equation~\ref{eq:eod}, which is the equal-opportunity difference of Hardt et~al.\ \cite{hardt2016}. Groups below \code{min\_group\_n} (30 in all runs) are excluded.

\subsection{Why the trigger is the TPR gap}\label{sec:l4}
The first TrustLens binary fairness path produced evidence but never a risk, so a fairness defect could not be flagged by construction (the fairness-rule limitation). Our first fix (\code{v5}, commit \code{d4268fb}) triggered on an ``excess demographic parity'' statistic. Before freezing it we inspected the stored evidence from the 2026-10-02 experiments and rejected it. The reason is algebraic. If $b_g = P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g)-P(Y{=}1\mid g)$ is the per-group bias of the positive rate, then
\begin{equation}
b_g = (\mathrm{TPR}_g-1)\,\pi_g + \mathrm{FPR}_g\,(1-\pi_g),\qquad \pi_g = P(Y{=}1\mid g),
\end{equation}
so $b_g$ depends directly on the group base rate $\pi_g$. In the stored data the statistic was non-zero on clean controls, fell as the injected defect grew, and rose with the safety defect. A trigger that does that cannot be defended.

We replaced it with the TPR gap (\code{v6}, tl-fairness-binary-v1.2). We have to be clear about the order of events: the TPR gap was chosen \emph{after} looking at that exploratory data, so it was not pre-registered in the strict sense, and the 2026-10-02 suite can no longer serve as a confirmatory test of it. Confirmation needed fresh data and a new seed, and that is the fairness confirmation experiment (Section~\ref{sec:l5}). The threshold $\varepsilon=0.02$ was inherited from the methodology-wide practical tolerance and was not re-tuned.

\subsection{Decision rule}
Table~\ref{tab:fairrule} gives the ordered decision rule, which is frozen in the code and in the pre-registration of the fairness confirmation run. The CI is a percentile bootstrap of EOD itself with $B=1000$ row resamples and a fixed seed (42 by default); a replicate in which a kept group has no positive label is discarded.

\noindent Two properties of this rule matter later. First, it is a \emph{disparity} rule, not a defect detector: a model with no injected defect but a real TPR gap will be flagged, and the status text says the flag is ``evidence for review, not a finding that the model is unfair''. Second, the width gate decides outcomes whenever the CI width is close to 0.15, which it was for most models in our data. We report that effect instead of re-tuning the gate. Base rates are kept in the evidence (\code{label\_rate\_gap}), because a large gap between groups' positive-label rates is the main reason that raw parity differences cannot be read as bias. For multiclass heads the probe uses a worst-group accuracy gap rule (\code{F-FAIR-PERF}, \code{tl-methodology-v1.0}); our experiments are all binary.

\section{Robustness}
\code{robustness\_nlp.py} replaces up to \code{max\_changes} alphanumeric characters per text (default 3, from an attack budget of 0.03) with random characters, using a seeded generator, and runs clean and perturbed text through the model. Evidence: clean accuracy, robust accuracy, paired accuracy drop with a bootstrap CI, attack success rate, perturbation coverage. The risk \code{R-ROB-PERT} is raised when the drop exceeds $\varepsilon_{drop}=0.05$ \emph{and} the CI lower bound does too. Gates stop the probe from scoring when fewer than 100 samples were evaluated, when too few labels are compatible with the model, when clean accuracy is below 0.20, when perturbation coverage is below 1.0 (\code{G-ROB-PERT-COVERAGE}), or when the CI of the accuracy drop is wider than 0.20 (\code{G-ROB-CI-WIDE}). The coverage gate caused the abstention that opened our severity sweep (Section~\ref{sec:l6}): a single row containing only the characters \code{:(} cannot be perturbed.

\paragraph{Constant predictors.} A model that answers one class for nearly every row is trivially stable and trivially fair, and scoring it would reward a broken evaluation. \code{prediction\_gate.py} flags a collapse when one class takes at least 98\% of predictions, and the fairness and robustness probes then refuse to score. The gate exists because our first experiment contained exactly such a model (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}).

\paragraph{Limits.} Random character swaps test typo sensitivity, not adversarial robustness; a gradient-guided attack \cite{ebrahimi2018} would be stronger. In every sweep we ran, the drop stayed below $\varepsilon_{drop}$, so \code{R-ROB-PERT} fired on only one real model (Section~\ref{sec:hub}). The probe produces graded evidence but almost never a risk.

\section{Safety}
Safety has two parts that the code keeps separate.

\textbf{Behavioural part.} The model runs on labelled rows that carry a \code{severe} marker (for us: Civil Comments rows with a severe-toxicity annotation above 0.0001). Among rows whose true label is positive, the probe computes the false-negative rate on the severe subset,
\begin{equation}
\text{severe FNR} = \frac{\#\{\text{severe, true positive label, predicted negative}\}}{\#\{\text{severe, true positive label}\}},
\end{equation}
with a Wilson interval, plus the overall FNR on positives, the ratio $\text{severe FNR}/\text{overall FNR}$ (\code{fnr\_ratio}), harmful recall and benign FPR. At least 30 severe positives are required (\code{min\_severe\_n}). Up to v9 the behavioural part produced evidence only, with no behavioural risk identifier, because we had no defensible absolute level at which a miss rate becomes a risk; so the deterministic risk layer never raised a safety risk in the studies of Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. Version v10 (\code{tl-safety-v1.2}) adds one named risk, \code{S-BEH-SEVERE-MISS}, which fires when the severe FNR and its Wilson lower bound both exceed 0.50 and the benign FPR is at most 0.30. The tolerance needs no reference model (missing over half of the severe rows is worse than chance), was declared before an offline replay (Section~\ref{sec:safety-v10}), and is exploratory.

\textbf{Card part.} \code{safety\_card.py} (\code{tl-safety-v1.1}) checks four disclosure items (misuse risks, privacy, security warnings, data disclosure) and flags high-impact deployment claims. Missing items are \emph{disclosure gaps}, not risks (Section~\ref{sec:gaps}).

\section{Integrity}\label{sec:integ}
Integrity in TrustLens has two roles: it records whether the model's identity and disclosure are well-specified (revision pinned, file listing recorded, licence declared), and it checks whether the bytes that were evaluated match a stated reference.

Up to \code{tl-integrity-v1.1} the probe hashed one weight file. That left three holes: sharded checkpoints were not verified at all, an edited \code{config.json} or tokenizer was invisible, and an extra weight file sitting next to the attested one went unnoticed. Version 1.2 (artifact integrity, ADR~0013) enumerates the \emph{artifact set} deterministically from top-level files: weights (single, or every shard named by the index file), loader files (\code{config.json}, \code{generation\_config.json}, tokenizer files) and any other weight-like file (hashed so that an unexpected one can be detected). The set is compared with the first authoritative manifest found: an operator-supplied manifest, a folder \code{artifact\_manifest.json}, or the legacy \code{train\_manifest.json} weight hash. For Hub models every weight shard is compared with its LFS sha256.

\paragraph{What this does and does not prove.} A VERIFIED status says that the bytes evaluated are the bytes a manifest attests. It does not show the model is safe (a backdoor would sit inside the attested bytes) or who produced the manifest, so it catches accidental or uncoordinated change, not a capable attacker. A mismatch shows that bytes differ, not that someone tampered (ADR~0013).

\section{Explainability}
In TrustLens, Explainability means \emph{documentation transparency}. \code{explainability\_card.py} searches the card for five required sections (intended use, limitations, training data, evaluation, ethical considerations), and the coverage ratio is the number present divided by five. Template placeholders such as ``[More Information Needed]'' are rejected by \code{card\_markdown.nontrivial()}. \code{explainability\_eval.py} also looks for contradictions such as \code{open\_claim\_vs\_restrictive\_license} and \code{no\_limitations\_but\_production\_claim}; these raise \code{E-DOC-CONTRADICTION}. A missing section is a disclosure gap (\code{E-DOC-INCOMPLETE}).

This probe cannot tell whether a human could understand \emph{why} the model made a prediction, and it reads headings, so a card with the right headings and no content can pass. Reviewers in the independent evidence review noticed this: on a skeleton card where 60\% of sections were detected, they saw empty headings and rated the evidence accordingly (Section~\ref{sec:l10}).

\section{Risks versus disclosure gaps}\label{sec:gaps}
In the controlled suite of 2026-10-02, the evidence-level detection rule flagged \emph{both} clean controls on Safety and Explainability (rate 1.00) and one on Integrity (0.50). Every one of those flags came from an \emph{absence}: a card section that was not there, or a local folder with no Hub revision. A defect detector that fires on every clean model is not detecting defects.

Version \code{v4-disclosure-gaps-2026} (disclosure-gap separation) therefore splits the two. \code{risks\_triggered} now holds only positive evidence of a problem: \code{E-DOC-CONTRADICTION}, \code{I-INT-BYTES-DIVERGE}, \code{I-INT-LISTING-DRIFT} and the metric-based risks. Five identifiers move to a separate \code{disclosure\_gaps} list: \code{S-GOV-DISCLOSURE-GAP}, \code{E-DOC-INCOMPLETE}, \code{I-INT-REV-UNPINNED}, \code{I-INT-MANIFEST-MISSING}, \code{I-INT-LICENSE-UNDISCLOSED}. When only gaps are present, \code{aspect\_scoring} is \code{disclosure\_gap}. The coverage ratios and flags are unchanged, so the card bands do not move; the LLM prompt still sees the gaps in its evidence block. Local model folders are now identified by the sha256 of their weight file (checked against \code{train\_manifest.json} when it records one), so a locally registered model is no longer ``unpinned by construction''.

The cost is stated in Section~\ref{sec:l3}: the split removed the false flags and, with them, some of the recall on injected card defects. It also means real Hub models, almost all of which have incomplete cards, now show up with gaps instead of risks (Section~\ref{sec:hub}).

\section{From evidence to O/S/D}\label{sec:osd}
\subsection{The deterministic engine}
The default engine never proposes O/S/D. Every aspect starts with $O=S=D=\code{None}$, FRIES is withheld, and a reviewer may supply any subset of the three. We kept this as the default because the project has no validated mapping from evidence to ratings.

\subsection{The heuristic engine, v3 bands}
\code{HeuristicOSDAgent} proposes ratings with documented bands, and every rationale it writes is prefixed ``[PROPOSED / REQUIRES VALIDATION]''. Proposals are clamped to $[1,9]$: 0 (veto) and 10 (optimal) are reserved for human judgment. With $\mathrm{scale}(x)=\mathrm{clamp}(\mathrm{round}(10x),1,9)$ the v3 bands are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fairness:} the gap is the larger of the parity term and the absolute equalized-odds difference; $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\mathrm{gap})$, one less on thin slices; $D=8$.
\item \textbf{Robustness:} $O=\mathrm{scale}(\min(\text{robust}/\text{clean},1))$, $S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\min(\text{drop}/0.10,1))$, $D=8$.
\item \textbf{Safety (behavioural):} $O=\mathrm{scale}(1-\text{severe FNR})$, $S=3$ if $\text{fnr\_ratio}>1.5$ else $6$, $D=8$.
\item \textbf{Integrity, Explainability, Safety (card):} $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(\text{pass rate or coverage})$, $D$ as coverage; through v10 an empty card got $(2,2,3)$, and from v11 it abstains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The calibrated mapping, v4}\label{sec:v4}
In the 2026-10-02 sweeps the evidence moved monotonically with injected severity (Spearman $\rho=1.0$), while the dimension score moved only 1.3--1.7 points over the full range of 0 to 100\% defect. The v3 bands lose information in several ways: for robustness $O$ was constant at 9, for safety $S$ was constant at 6, and a coarse $\mathrm{scale}(1-\text{gap})$ made adjacent levels tie.

The calibrated mapping replaces the level computation for three evidence families with a range normalisation. For evidence $e$ (Fairness: the gap; Robustness: the accuracy drop; Safety: the severe FNR) with two anchors $a$ (expected evidence of a defect-free model) and $b$ (expected evidence at the largest calibrated defect),
\begin{equation}
x=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{e-a}{b-a},0,1\right),\qquad O=S=\left\lfloor 9-8x+\tfrac12\right\rfloor,\label{eq:v4}
\end{equation}
so $O=S\in[1,9]$. $D$, the abstention logic and the card bands stay as in v3. The anchors are the intercept and the intercept plus slope of an ordinary-least-squares fit of $e$ on normalised severity over a \emph{calibration split only}: 3\,000 evaluation rows plus an 8\,000-row clean training slice from Civil Comments, seven models with nested flips at 0.3, 0.6 and 1.0 for Fairness and Safety, and attack budgets 0.02--0.10 for Robustness, all under seed 43 and disjoint by exact text from the suite and the confirmatory data. Because the held-out fairness confirmation and severity sweeps values were already known, the pre-declaration fixed everything except the two anchors per family in advance.

\noindent The v4 scale is \emph{relative to the largest injected defect}. A robustness accuracy drop of three percentage points maps to $O=S=1$, the same level as a total failure would, because three points is where our calibration sweep ended. The mapping says ``as bad as the worst defect we injected'' and nothing about absolute harm. The robustness calibration also saturates, so the top held-out level is clipped (Section~\ref{sec:l1}). Since $O=S$ by construction, the Severity rating adds no independent information; $D$ added none either in the three families.

\subsection{Evidence guards, v9}
After round~3, a set of audit fixes added three guards (\code{v9-evidence-guards-2026}), all in the heuristic engine. (1) If artifact verification reports MISMATCH, Integrity gets $O=S=1$; before this, the band read the pass rate of the metadata checks, which a tampered folder could still pass. (2) Safety abstains when benign FPR exceeds 0.30, a competence floor so that a model flagging everything does not look safe by its miss rate. This threshold was set \emph{after} we saw the external Hub run, it is marked PROPOSED in the code, and it is not pre-registered. (3) Detectability becomes $D=\mathrm{clamp}(8-\lfloor w/0.05\rfloor)$ for a CI of width $w$ (the EOD interval for Fairness, the clean-accuracy interval for Robustness, the severe-FNR interval for Safety) instead of a constant 8. None of the three guards has been validated on fresh data.

\subsection{The LLM-assisted engine}
For Integrity, Explainability and Safety, \code{llm\_v1} sends one batched prompt (\code{osd-llm-v3-decision-first-2026-10-03}) to a hosted model and asks for O, S, D from 1 to 9 with a short rationale. Three hosted providers (A, B, C) are tried in order, with temperature 0.2. The first provider to return a valid, parseable answer wins; if all fail, the heuristic values are kept and tagged \code{heuristic\_fallback}. The prompt caps card text at 5\,000 characters and evidence at 3\,000; since pipeline hardening the decision fields sit on an untruncated first line (Table~\ref{tab:bugs}). The engine is an experimental comparison engine; the LLM comparison study (Section~\ref{sec:l2}) shows why it should not be treated as a validated rater.

\subsection{Human review}
In \code{AI\_ASSISTED} mode the pipeline stops at \code{AWAITING\_REVIEW}; \code{osd/\allowbreak review.py} merges the reviewer's edits (possibly only some of O, S, D) over the proposal and records the overrides.
