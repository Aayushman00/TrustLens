\chapter{FRIES Methodology}\label{ch:fries}

This chapter defines what \textbf{TrustLens} measures and how evidence becomes \textbf{risk} decisions, ratings and scores, as used in the experiments. Only the method variants needed to read a result are named. Version history and rule identifiers are in \code{docs/REPRODUCING\_THESIS\_RESULTS.md}.

\section{Four stages between a measurement and a score}\label{sec:stages}
A measurement becomes a score in four separate steps, which the experiments of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} keep apart.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Measured evidence} is what a \textbf{probe} computed from the model and the data: an accuracy, a gap in true-positive rates with its interval, a set of file hashes, the share of card sections present. It is a number or a status and carries no judgment.
\item \textbf{Risk findings and documentation gaps} are decisions taken from the evidence by fixed rules with stated thresholds. The outcome is one of: a \emph{risk} (positive evidence of a problem), a \emph{disclosure gap} (information that should be present is missing), no material finding, or \emph{insufficient evidence}, in which case the later stages are skipped.
\item \textbf{Proposed ratings} are the \textbf{O/S/D} triples that an engine suggests or a \textbf{reviewer} enters. They are proposals; the report labels them as requiring validation.
\item \textbf{Dimension and aggregate scores} are computed from the ratings by the \textbf{FRIES} formula (Equations~\ref{eq:pi} and~\ref{eq:fries}) and exist only where a complete triple exists.
\end{enumerate}

A change at one stage does not automatically propagate to the next: a parity gap that rises from 0.11 to 0.21 changes the evidence at every step, changes the risk finding only when the rule's \textbf{threshold} is crossed (Table~\ref{tab:fairrule}), and changes the \textbf{rating} only if the mapping assigns a different integer.

\section{From ratings to scores}
\textbf{FRIES} \cite{rutinowski2024} adapts \textbf{FMEA}. It covers five dimensions: \emph{Fairness} (does behaviour differ between groups of texts), \emph{Robustness} (does it survive small typos), \emph{Integrity} (are the evaluated files and the model's identity what is claimed), \emph{Explainability} (does the documentation explain the model) and \emph{Safety} (does the model miss severe harmful content, and does its card disclose \textbf{safety} limits). For each relevant risk an analyst rates \emph{Occurrence} (O: how unlikely the failure is), \emph{Severity} (S: how mild its consequences would be) and \emph{Detection} (D: how easily the failure would be noticed before it causes harm). TrustLens uses an inverted convention in which \textbf{higher is more favourable on all three scales}, so $9/9/8$ is a favourable rating. Conventional FMEA ranks the other way, with high numbers for frequent, severe or hard-to-detect failures. This convention applies to every engine in the experiments. It does not mean any engine produces validated risk estimates (Section~\ref{sec:osd}). The scorer requires integers in $0..10$ and
\begin{align}
\Pi_r &= \sqrt[3]{O_r\, S_r\, D_r}, \qquad \Pi_r = 0 \text{ if any of } O_r,S_r,D_r = 0 \text{ (veto)},\label{eq:pi}\\
T_a &= \frac{1}{|R_a|}\sum_{r\in R_a}\Pi_r, \qquad
\text{FRIES} = \sum_{a} w_a\, T_a, \quad w_a = 0.2 \text{ by default.}\label{eq:fries}
\end{align}
In every experiment each dimension has a single triple, so $T_a=\Pi_r$ and weights are equal. The \textbf{geometric mean} lets one low component dominate. The rule-based engine proposes only $1..9$ (0 and 10 are reserved for human judgment), so its dimension scores lie between $1.0$ and $9.0$. In set terms, let $\mathcal A=\{\text{Fairness},\text{Robustness},\text{Integrity},\text{Explainability},\text{Safety}\}$ and $\mathcal W\subseteq\mathcal A$ the dimensions withheld for lack of a complete triple. The aggregate uses only the rated dimensions and exists only when none is withheld:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal R=\mathcal A\setminus\mathcal W,\qquad \text{FRIES is defined}\iff \mathcal W=\varnothing,\label{eq:rated}
\end{equation}
so a withheld dimension is never replaced by a default value.

\section{Evidence: the five dimensions}
\subsection{Fairness}\label{sec:fair}
For a binary classifier with favourable class $\hat y=1$ and groups $g$ defined by a sensitive column, the \textbf{probe} computes the \emph{demographic-parity difference} (DPD), the \textbf{gap between groups} in the share of texts predicted positive, and, for each group, the \emph{true-positive rate} (TPR), the share of actual positives that are predicted positive. The gap in TPR between groups is the \emph{equal-opportunity difference} (EOD):
\begin{align}
\mathrm{DPD} &= \max_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g)-\min_g P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g),\\
\mathrm{TPR}_g &= \tfrac{TP_g}{TP_g+FN_g}, \qquad \mathrm{EOD} = \max_g \mathrm{TPR}_g-\min_g \mathrm{TPR}_g.\label{eq:eod}\\
\mathrm{FPR}_g &= \tfrac{FP_g}{FP_g+TN_g}, \qquad \mathrm{EOD}_{\mathrm{odds}} = \max\bigl\{\mathrm{EOD},\ \max_g \mathrm{FPR}_g-\min_g \mathrm{FPR}_g\bigr\}.\label{eq:odds}
\end{align}
The \textbf{probe} also reports the corresponding gap in false-positive rates (FPR, the share of actual negatives predicted positive) and the gap in label rates $P(Y{=}1\mid g)$. EOD is the \textbf{equal-opportunity} difference of Hardt et~al.\ \cite{hardt2016}. It is the primary \textbf{fairness} quantity and is distinct from the larger equalized-odds difference (the larger of the TPR and FPR gaps). Groups smaller than 30 are excluded.

The same quantities can be written with sets. Let $D_g$ be the evaluated rows of group $g$ (pairwise disjoint), $P$ the rows whose true label is positive, $N=\overline{P}$ the remaining rows, and $\hat P$ the rows the model predicts positive. Then
\begin{align}
P(\hat Y{=}1\mid g) &= \frac{|D_g\cap \hat P|}{|D_g|}, &
\mathrm{TPR}_g &= \frac{|D_g\cap P\cap \hat P|}{|D_g\cap P|},\\
\mathrm{FPR}_g &= \frac{|D_g\cap N\cap \hat P|}{|D_g\cap N|}, &
\mathrm{EOD} &= \max_{g,g'}\bigl|\mathrm{TPR}_g-\mathrm{TPR}_{g'}\bigr|.\label{eq:fairset}
\end{align}

\emph{Why the trigger is the TPR gap.} A first rule based on ``excess'' demographic parity depended on group base rates $P(Y{=}1\mid g)$: it was non-zero on clean controls and shrank as the \textbf{injected defect} grew. The TPR gap conditions on the true label. It was chosen after inspecting \textbf{exploratory} data, so it was confirmed on fresh data with a new seed (Section~\ref{sec:fairconf}). The tolerance $\varepsilon=0.02$, the largest TPR gap treated as negligible, is a method-wide practical tolerance and was not tuned.

Uncertainty is expressed as a 95\% \emph{confidence interval} (CI), an interval produced by a procedure designed to cover the true value in 95\% of repeated samples (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). Here it is a percentile bootstrap of EOD that re-samples the evaluation rows $B=1000$ times (seed 42). It reflects row resampling on one evaluation set, not retraining of the model. With $\mathrm{EOD}^{*}_b$ the value on the $b$-th resample, the interval is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CI}_{95}=\bigl[\,q_{0.025}(\mathrm{EOD}^{*}_1,\dots,\mathrm{EOD}^{*}_B),\ q_{0.975}(\mathrm{EOD}^{*}_1,\dots,\mathrm{EOD}^{*}_B)\bigr],\qquad B=1000,\label{eq:bootci}
\end{equation}
where $q_p$ is the $p$-quantile. The decision rule (Table~\ref{tab:fairrule}) is a \emph{disparity} rule, not a defect detector: a model with a real TPR gap but no injected defect is flagged, and the report calls this ``evidence for review''. The width rule often decides the outcome because most intervals in our data have width near 0.15. A rating withheld this way is not a pass.

\subsection{Robustness}
The \textbf{probe} replaces up to 3 alphanumeric characters per text (\textbf{attack} budget 0.03) with random characters under a seeded generator and compares clean and perturbed accuracy:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{drop}=\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{clean}}-\mathrm{Acc}_{\mathrm{pert}},\qquad
\text{risk}\iff \mathrm{drop}>\varepsilon_{drop}\ \wedge\ \mathrm{CI}_{\mathrm{lower}}(\mathrm{drop})>\varepsilon_{drop},\label{eq:drop}
\end{equation}
with $\varepsilon_{drop}=0.05$. Let $C_{\mathrm{clean}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{pert}}$ be the sets of rows classified correctly before and after perturbation, over the same $n$ rows. Since $\mathrm{Acc}=|C|/n$, the drop depends only on the rows whose outcome changed:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{drop}=\frac{|C_{\mathrm{clean}}\setminus C_{\mathrm{pert}}|-|C_{\mathrm{pert}}\setminus C_{\mathrm{clean}}|}{n},\qquad
\mathrm{coverage}=\frac{|T_{\mathrm{pert}}|}{|T|},\label{eq:dropset}
\end{equation}
where $T$ is the set of evaluated texts and $T_{\mathrm{pert}}\subseteq T$ those that could be perturbed. Evidence: clean and robust accuracy, \textbf{paired accuracy drop} with bootstrap CI, and \textbf{perturbation} coverage (the share of texts that could be perturbed). A \textbf{robustness} risk is raised when the drop exceeds $\varepsilon_{drop}=0.05$ \emph{and} the CI lower bound does too. A rating is \textbf{withheld} when fewer than 100 samples are evaluated, clean accuracy is below 0.20, perturbation coverage is below 1.0, or the CI of the drop is wider than 0.20. A \emph{constant-predictor gate} refuses to score \textbf{fairness} and \textbf{robustness} when one class receives at least 98\% of predictions, since a broken model is trivially stable. The probe tests typo sensitivity, not adversarial robustness.

\subsection{Safety}\label{sec:safety}
The behavioural part measures how often the model misses \emph{severe} positives: comments whose true label is toxic and whose annotator-averaged severe-toxicity score (dataset column \code{severe\_toxicity}) is above 0.0001. The \emph{severe false-negative rate} (severe FNR) is the share of these comments the model fails to flag as toxic:
\begin{equation}
\text{severe FNR} = \frac{\#\{\text{severe, true positive, predicted negative}\}}{\#\{\text{severe, true positive}\}},
\end{equation}
Equivalently, with $S\subseteq P$ the set of severe rows and $\hat N$ the rows predicted negative, $\text{severe FNR}=|S\cap \hat N|/|S|$. It is reported with a \textbf{Wilson interval} (a \textbf{confidence interval} for a proportion that stays reliable for small counts), together with the overall \textbf{false-negative rate} (FNR), their ratio, harmful recall and benign FPR. For $k$ misses among $n$ severe positives, with $\hat p=k/n$ and $z=1.96$, the Wilson interval is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\hat p+\frac{z^{2}}{2n}\ \pm\ z\sqrt{\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}{n}+\frac{z^{2}}{4n^{2}}}}{1+\frac{z^{2}}{n}}.\label{eq:wilson}
\end{equation}
At least 30 severe positives are required. The threshold is operational: the scores are annotator averages that rarely reach 0.5, so ``above zero'' leaves enough rows. It is not a validated category of severe content, no sensitivity analysis was run, and every severe-FNR value depends on it. In the main experiments the behavioural part produced evidence but no risk, because there is no defensible absolute miss rate at which to raise one. An exploratory severe-miss rule (severe FNR and its \textbf{Wilson lower bound} both above 0.50, benign FPR at most 0.30) was added later and is evaluated only by replay (Section~\ref{sec:l6}). The card part checks four disclosure items (\textbf{misuse}, \textbf{privacy}, security, data). Missing items are \textbf{disclosure gaps}.

\subsection{Integrity}\label{sec:integ}
\textbf{Integrity} records whether the model's identity is well specified (exact model version pinned, file listing recorded, \textbf{licence} declared) and whether the bytes evaluated match a stated reference. The \textbf{probe} lists the model's complete \emph{file set} (weights or every shard named by the index, config, generation config, tokenizer files and any other weight-like file) and compares it with the first authoritative \emph{manifest} available, that is, a list of expected files with their reference hashes (operator-supplied, a folder \textbf{manifest}, or a legacy weight hash). For Hub models every file in the \textbf{manifest} is compared with its published sha256. The check covers only the files the \textbf{manifest} names. Let $M$ be the set of files in the manifest, $F$ the set of files of the evaluated model with $F_w\subseteq F$ its weight files, and $h$, $h_{\mathrm{ref}}$ the computed and reference hashes. Then
\begin{align}
\text{missing}&=M\setminus F, \qquad \text{unlisted}=F_w\setminus M,\\
\text{mismatch}&=\{f\in M\cap F : h(f)\neq h_{\mathrm{ref}}(f)\},\label{eq:fileset}
\end{align}
and the model is verified exactly when $\text{missing}\cup\text{unlisted}\cup\text{mismatch}=\varnothing$. Outcomes: verified (all match; no risk); mismatch, including an unlisted \textbf{weight file} (a byte-mismatch risk). Incomplete (an expected file is missing; rating \textbf{withheld}). Unpinned or unsupported (nothing trusted to compare with; not a risk, hash not compared). In the external \textbf{audit} of Chapter~\ref{ch:results} the stored \textbf{manifest} of each Hub model held exactly one \textbf{weight file}, the one the \textbf{worker} loaded (\code{model.safetensors} or \code{pytorch\_model.bin}). Other weight formats in the same repositories, such as a second serialisation or an ONNX export, were not hashed. A match shows that the evaluated bytes are the bytes a \textbf{manifest} attests. It does not show who made the \textbf{manifest}, nor that the model is benign, safe or free of \textbf{backdoors}, and a mismatch shows that bytes differ, not that anyone tampered.

\subsection{Explainability}
\textbf{Explainability} means \emph{documentation transparency}. The \textbf{probe} checks the \textbf{model card} for five required sections (intended use, limitations, training data, evaluation, \textbf{ethical} considerations). Coverage is the share present, and template placeholders such as ``[More Information Needed]'' do not count. With $R$ the set of five required sections and $Q\subseteq R$ those found with real content,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{coverage}=\frac{|R\cap Q|}{|R|},\qquad G=R\setminus Q,\label{eq:gapset}
\end{equation}
where $G$ is the set of disclosure gaps. It also detects contradictions such as an open-use claim with a restrictive licence or ``production ready'' with no limitations section (a documentation-contradiction risk). It cannot tell whether a human could understand \emph{why} the model predicted something, and a card with the right headings and empty content can pass the section check.

\section{Risks versus disclosure gaps}\label{sec:gaps}
In the first controlled suite, a \textbf{detection} rule based directly on the measured results flagged both clean controls on \textbf{Safety} and \textbf{Explainability} (rate 1.00) and one on \textbf{Integrity} (0.50). Each flag came from an \emph{absence}: a missing card section, or a local folder with no Hub revision. A detector that fires on every clean model detects nothing. The method therefore keeps two lists. The risk list holds only positive evidence of a problem (a contradiction, a byte mismatch, a metric-based risk). Missing documentation goes to a separate \emph{disclosure-gap} list (missing \textbf{safety} disclosure, incomplete card, unpinned revision, missing \textbf{manifest}, undisclosed licence), and when only gaps exist the dimension is reported as a \textbf{disclosure gap}. A gap can matter for deployment even when no defect has been shown. The split makes the distinction visible and, as Section~\ref{sec:gapres} shows, costs recall on injected card defects.

\section{From evidence to O/S/D ratings}\label{sec:osd}
\textbf{What the three numbers mean in the implementation.} In FMEA an analyst rates three things independently. The TrustLens engines are simpler. For the behavioural dimensions, $O$ and $S$ both come from the same evidence statistic (how far the measurement lies from a defect-free value) and are equal in the \textbf{range-scaled} mapping. $D$ is not how noticeable a failure would be to a user, which the project cannot measure. After the evidence guards it falls as the confidence interval widens. For the card-based dimensions, the three numbers come from card coverage or, in the LLM-assisted engine, from a model prompted to rate each of them. A dimension score is therefore a transformed evidence statistic with an uncertainty discount, not three independent expert judgments.

\textbf{Deterministic engine (default).} It proposes no ratings: O, S and D stay empty and \textbf{FRIES} is \textbf{withheld} unless a reviewer supplies them, because the project has no validated evidence-to-rating mapping.

\textbf{Rule-based engine, original scoring bands.} With the scaling function
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{scale}(x)=\mathrm{clamp}\bigl(\mathrm{round}(10x),\,1,\,9\bigr),\label{eq:scale}
\end{equation}
the ratings are: \textbf{Fairness} $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\mathrm{gap})$ with the gap the larger of DPD and the equalized-odds difference, $D=8$; \textbf{Robustness} $O=\mathrm{scale}(\min(\text{robust}/\text{clean},1))$, $S=\mathrm{scale}(1-\min(\text{drop}/0.10,1))$, $D=8$. Behavioural \textbf{Safety} $O=\mathrm{scale}(1-\text{severe FNR})$, $S=3$ if the FNR ratio exceeds 1.5 and $6$ otherwise, $D=8$. Card-based \textbf{Integrity}, \textbf{Explainability} and \textbf{Safety} $O=S=\mathrm{scale}(\text{pass rate or coverage})$.

\textbf{Range-scaled mapping.}\label{sec:v4} In the first sweeps the evidence tracked injected \textbf{severity} but the dimension score under the original bands moved only 1.3--1.7 points, because the bands lose information (\textbf{robustness} $O$ stayed at 9, \textbf{safety} $S$ at 6). For \textbf{Fairness} (evidence $e$ = EOD), \textbf{Robustness} (accuracy drop) and \textbf{Safety} (severe FNR), the \textbf{range-scaled mapping} rescales linearly between two anchors $a$ (expected evidence of a defect-free model) and $b$ (expected evidence at the largest defect used for fitting):
\begin{equation}
x=\mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{e-a}{b-a},0,1\right),\qquad O=S=\left\lfloor 9-8x+\tfrac12\right\rfloor\in[1,9],\label{eq:v4}
\end{equation}
with $D$, the withholding rules and the card bands as in the original bands. The anchors (Table~\ref{tab:anchors}, Figure~\ref{fig:mapping}) are the intercept and intercept plus slope of a least-squares line of evidence against defect severity on a separate fitting split (Section~\ref{sec:calib}), fixed before evaluation.

\noindent The scale is \emph{relative to the largest injected defect}: a \textbf{robustness} drop of three percentage points maps to $O=S=1$, the same as a total failure, because the fitting sweep ended there. Since $O=S$ by construction and $D$ is constant, each dimension is one evidence statistic turned into one number, with no external \textbf{ground truth}. The map is linear between the two anchors and saturates at 9 or 1 beyond them. The anchors come from one fitting split, one base model and one training seed per level, with injected \textbf{label flips} and character swaps rather than real defects. The three families are scaled independently, so a \textbf{Fairness} 5 and a \textbf{Safety} 5 do not describe equal risk. It supports one claim, tested in Chapter~\ref{ch:results}: it separates the synthetic defect levels we tested. It is not calibrated to real-world harm.

\textbf{Later evidence guards.} Three guards followed the first real-model run: a byte mismatch forces \textbf{Integrity} $O=S=1$; \textbf{Safety} is \textbf{withheld} when the benign FPR exceeds 0.30, and $D=\mathrm{clamp}(8-\lfloor w/0.05\rfloor)$ instead of a constant 8, where $w$ is the width of the evidence interval (for \textbf{Robustness}, the clean-accuracy interval). The FPR ceiling was set after the external audit; no guard is validated on fresh data. The controlled suite and sweeps (Sections~\ref{sec:repeat}--\ref{sec:l6}) used the original bands (v3), the scoring evaluation the range-scaled mapping (v4), and the later results (Sections~\ref{sec:l7}--\ref{sec:hub}) the final method with the guards (v9).

\textbf{LLM-assisted engine.} For \textbf{Integrity}, \textbf{Explainability} and \textbf{Safety} (card part) one batched prompt (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}) asks a hosted model for O, S, D in $1..9$ and a rationale. On total failure the rule-based ratings stay, tagged \emph{fallback}. Its limits are in Section~\ref{sec:l2}.
